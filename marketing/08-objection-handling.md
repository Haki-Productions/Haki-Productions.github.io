# Objection Handling — The Tiger's Response Library

**Owner:** The Tiger  
**Purpose:** Structured, human responses for the four most common objections. Never ad-lib these — use the exact words.

---

## 1. "Too expensive."

**Your response:** *"What is the one thing you need most?"*

Then: Offer the smallest project that solves that one thing. Reduce scope, not quality.

**Examples:**
- If they need a lookbook but not a full film → offer just The Press Kit (€750)
- If they need a short film but not a retainer → offer The Engine (€1,950/mo)
- If they need an event recap but not 3 hours → offer a shorter edit

**Never:** Discount the price without reducing scope. That leads to "busy and broke."

---

## 2. "I need to think about it."

**Your response:** *"What is the specific thing you are unsure about?"*

Then: Isolate the real objection. Is it price? Trust? Timeline? Scope? Address that one thing directly.

**Never:** Say "No problem, just let me know." That resets the conversation and often means they'll never circle back.

---

## 3. "Can you do it cheaper?"

**Your response:** *"I can reduce scope, not quality. Here is what we cut."*

Then: Be specific about what disappears at a lower price point. Show the trade.

**Examples:**
- "Fewer verticals, shorter film."
- "One shoot day instead of two."
- "Fewer photos, no drone."
- "No revision rounds beyond the included number."

**Never:** Say "Maybe" or "We'll see." That's a trap. Give a concrete scope reduction.

---

## 4. "I found someone cheaper."

**Your response:** *"Good. Compare what they deliver for that price. If they do the same thing for less, take it. If they do less, you are not comparing the same thing."*

Then: Prompt them to list exactly what the other person offers, and walk through the differences item by item.

**Never:** Get defensive or start a price war. The Tiger does not compete on price — The Tiger competes on the right scope for the right price.

---

## 5. General principles

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Never discount without scope reduction** | A lower price with the same promise is how the business ends up busy and broke. |
| **Lead with the question** | Every objection has a question behind it. Find the question, answer it. |
| **Be concrete** | "I can reduce scope" is better than "We'll see." Specificity builds trust. |
| **Walk away when needed** | If a prospect only wants the lowest price and won't accept any scope reduction, they are not The Tiger's client. |
