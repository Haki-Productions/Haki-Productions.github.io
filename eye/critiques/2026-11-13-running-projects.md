# Running Projects — Critique

**Date:** 13 November 2026
**Work reviewed:** Haki agency site (8 pages), GATEKEEPERS MAG Issue 01, Toolbox, Links page, Soul Terror store status
**Brief in one line:** Turn attention into paid work at €1,000/month, starting with the agency site.
**One-line answer:** The build is good enough to sell. The project mix is the problem: eight build projects, no sell project.

**Verdict:** Fix the site. Rethink the portfolio.
**Score:** 76/100 (site as a commercial asset)

---

## First read

Three seconds on the home page: strong. Oversized type, real Soul Terror imagery, a clear promise, two obvious actions. It reads like a crew with a point of view, not an agency template.

Thirty seconds into the folder: the site has grown from the planned five pages to eight. Magazine, Toolbox and Links were added while the domain, form, booking and deployment are still unchecked. The work is good. The order of operations is wrong.

## What is working

1. **The brand world is real.** Ink, paper, hot pink, Anton, Space Mono, 1px lines, index numbers. It is consistent across all eight pages. Do not let anyone sand this down.
2. **The offers are concrete and priced.** Five named packages with deliverables, timelines and starting prices. That alone puts Haki ahead of most small agencies.
3. **The honesty is a feature.** Work page labels in-house, in-development and client work separately. No invented case studies. That is trust capital. Keep it.

---

## What must change

| Priority | Problem | Fix | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing is deployed. README launch checklist still open. | Register domain, push to Cloudflare Pages/Vercel, point DNS. One day. | A site nobody can reach sells nothing. |
| 2 | Inquiry form only opens the visitor's email app. No Tally/Formspree, no Cal.com. | Wire a real form endpoint + booking link. Keep mailto as fallback. | Every mailto step loses a percentage of serious buyers. |
| 3 | "Booking Q1 2027" on home and links. | Replace with real availability ("Taking 2 projects for December" or remove). | It signals full capacity while the goal is the first paid client. |
| 4 | Contact page Instagram points to `@soulterror00`, the streetwear label. | Point to a Haki account or remove the row until one exists. | A client ready to book lands on a hoodie brand and hesitates. |
| 5 | No analytics. | Add Plausible or Umami before outreach starts. | Without it you cannot tell a traffic problem from an offer problem. |
| 6 | Toolbox templates are mailto requests. | Swap to an email-capture form delivering the file automatically. | The plan wanted a lead magnet. Right now it creates work and captures nothing. |
| 7 | No paid client case study. | Ship one real client project, even small, with permission. | In-house proof proves taste. Client proof proves you can be hired. |
| 8 | "Five fronts" is the headline on home, services, about and work. | Use it once. Give the other pages their own angle. | Repetition flattens four pages into one. |
| 9 | Magazine, Toolbox and Links compete for the same attention. | Keep Magazine as proof, Toolbox as lead capture, Links as utility. Stop adding pages. | Every new page is a week not spent selling. |

**The pattern:** every project running is a making project. None of them is a selling project. The plan says 20 warm conversations → 5 proposals → 2 closes. That is the project with the highest return and the least evidence of activity.

---

## The killer note

**Stop building and start shipping.** Freeze new pages, including Headquarters. Spend one day wiring the form, booking and analytics, one day deploying, then put the site in front of 20 warm contacts. The site is at 76. The portfolio is at risk of staying at zero revenue while it climbs to 85.

---

## Next test

Send the live URL to five warm contacts with one question: "Would you book a call from this page, yes or no, and what stopped you?" Their answer is worth more than another page.
